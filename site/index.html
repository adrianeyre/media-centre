Why Media Centre

What Media Centre is

Every Appliance in the home has its own remote or its own maker's app, each with its own account, and nothing shows the whole home in one place.

Media Centre puts them in one app you run yourself, grouped by the Rooms of your home: a tab each for the TVs, air conditioners, lights, heating, the network, Amazon Echos and Ring devices, and the Rooms on your own floor plan. Watching a film from the NAS no longer means the TV's own media browser and its physical remote: choose it on your phone, and the Hub wakes the TV, opens its Player and converts only what the TV can't play.

Anyone on your home network can pick it up, as anyone in the room can pick up a remote. It isn't reachable from outside your home.

  • The Hub

    The always-on process, in Docker on a NAS or on a PC, that serves the Remote, controls every Appliance and serves your Media Files.

  • The Remote

    The web app you use from any phone, tablet or computer on the home network.

  • The Player

    The app the Hub installs on each Samsung TV to play your films, shows and music.

  • Rooms

    Every Appliance and device in its Room, on a floor plan drawn on your own image.

How a film reaches the TV

  1. 1

    You choose it

    In Watch & Listen, from a Media Source: a folder on the Hub's machine, or on a PC or NAS.

  2. 2

    The Hub wakes the TV

    With Wake-on-LAN if it is off, and waits for it to come on.

  3. 3

    It opens the Player

    The Player connects back to the Hub, which sends it the Media File.

  4. 4

    It picks a Route

    It reads the file with ffprobe and takes the cheapest way that the TV can play, converting with ffmpeg only what it can't.

  5. 5

    Every Remote follows it

    Now Showing shows the position, the audio and subtitle tracks and a frame from the film, and the Hub remembers where you stopped.

Features

Everything in the home, in one Remote

A tab for each kind of Appliance, every one a board of widgets you can move, resize or hide, on each phone, tablet and computer.

  • Samsung TVs

    Everything the TV's own remote does, from your phone: Power, the D-pad and keys, a number pad, the coloured and media keys, the volume and mute, typing into the TV's on-screen keyboard, and its apps, to launch or close. TV Menu mode drives the TV's own settings menu, with one-tap shortcuts, and Now Showing says which app is in front.

  • Your films, shows and music on the TV

    Browse your PC's or NAS's folders and play on the TV through its own Player: every format ffmpeg can decode, with audio tracks, subtitles (sidecar files too), music with its cover art, a folder played as a Queue with shuffle and repeat, and Resume Positions kept through restarts.

  • Midea air conditioners

    Power, mode, target temperature, fan speed, swing, Eco, Turbo and Sleep, with the room and outdoor temperatures, controlled on your home network with no cloud once each is added.

  • Lepro lights and Light Scenes

    On and off, brightness, white and colour, a Room's Lights at once, and Light Scenes to save and recall, with a film Light Scene for each TV. Built and tested against a fake Lepro Light: not yet tried on a real one.

  • Hive heating and hot water

    Each Heating zone and Radiator Valve's target and mode, the Hot Water, a Boost for each, and the Whole home at once, through Hive's cloud. Signing in to Hive is built from the library Home Assistant uses, and not yet tried against Hive itself.

  • Amazon Echo

    What each Echo is playing, its volume and Do Not Disturb, Ask Alexa, Say it and Announce, routines, music, alarms, timers and reminders, through Amazon's cloud.

  • Ring doorbells, cameras and chimes

    Snapshots, live video, recent events and recordings, the light, siren and motion detection, and a chime's volume and sounds, through Ring's cloud.

  • The network and Bluetooth

    Every device on your home network, who made it and the ports it is open on, and the Bluetooth devices near the Hub, each to name and put in a Room.

  • Rooms on your own floor plan

    Tap a Room on the plan for everything in it: its TVs, air conditioners, lights and heating, its Echos and Ring devices, and the other devices on the network. Draw the plan, floor by floor, on your own image of your home.

  • For everyone in the house

    It meets WCAG 2.2 at Level AA, works by keyboard and screen reader, follows your device's light or dark mode, has six colour themes, and installs to a phone's home screen.

Before you start

Requirements

One always-on machine for the Hub, on the same network as your TVs, and the Appliances you have. Each kind of Appliance is optional.

  • Docker with host networking on a NAS or mini PC running Linux: the Hub's image has Node, ffmpeg and ffprobe built in. Docker Desktop on Windows or macOS can't put it on your home network, so there use pnpm start.

  • Or, on a PC: Node 26 or later and pnpm 12, with ffmpeg and ffprobe (built with libx264) on the PATH, and Git.

  • The same subnet as your TVs. Finding TVs, switching one on with Wake-on-LAN and reading its volume don't cross a router, so not a separate VLAN or guest Wi-Fi.

  • An x86 (Intel or AMD) machine, ideally. Samsung's tools that install the Player are made for amd64 only: on an ARM NAS, install the Player from a PC with Tizen Studio instead. An Intel mini PC's GPU can do Full Conversion, too.

  • Samsung smart TVs. The Player runs on 2018 and later models, installed in Developer Mode with a Samsung signing certificate, made with Tizen Studio.

  • Your films, shows and music in folders the Hub can read: on its own machine, or mounted from a PC or NAS.

  • For the other tabs: Midea air conditioners (a v3 unit asks for your MSmartHome login once), Lepro lights, and Hive, Amazon or Ring accounts, which need the internet.

  • Any phone, tablet or computer with a browser, on your home network, for the Remote.

Five minutes

Quick start

Start the Hub, then open the Remote in a browser at port 41080. Choose where the Hub runs.

Docker on a NAS

On a Linux NAS or mini PC with Docker, on the same subnet as your TVs. Mount each folder of films, shows or music, read-only, and add it in Settings by its path inside the container. 

git clone {{REPO_URL}}.git
cd {{REPO_NAME}}
docker build -t media-centre-hub .
docker run -d --name media-centre-hub --network host \
  -v media-centre-data:/data \
  -v /volume1/video:/media/video:ro \
  --restart unless-stopped media-centre-hub

Then open http://<the NAS's address>:41080 on your phone.

A PC

With Node 26 or later, pnpm 12, and ffmpeg and ffprobe on the PATH. Any folder the Hub can read can be a Media Source. 

git clone {{REPO_URL}}.git
cd {{REPO_NAME}}
pnpm install
pnpm start   # builds the Remote and starts the Hub

Then open http://localhost:41080.

Set up your home

In Settings, add your Rooms, then "Find TVs" and allow the Remote on each TV, add your Media Sources, and find your air conditioners and lights. Sign in to Hive in Settings, and to Amazon and Ring on their tabs.

Install the Player

Put the TV in Developer Mode with the Hub's address, give Settings your Samsung signing certificate, then choose "Install / update Player" in the TV's Developer section.

Accessibility

First edition, effective 26 October 2026.

Conformance status: this website is built to meet the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2 at Level AA.

What we have done

  • Keyboard access: every link, button, tab and dialog works with a keyboard alone, with a clear focus outline the sticky header never hides. A "Skip to content" link leads the page.
  • Screen readers: one main heading, headings in order and labelled landmarks; every control has a name, every screenshot a description, and copying a command is announced.
  • Colour and contrast: text has at least 4.5:1 contrast with its background, and controls, icons and focus outlines at least 3:1, in light and dark mode, which follow your device. Windows Contrast Themes are respected.
  • Motion: transitions are short, and smooth scrolling and animation are switched off when your device asks for reduced motion.
  • Zoom and reflow: the page reflows down to 320 pixels wide and at 400% zoom without scrolling sideways.
  • Target sizes: every control is at least 24 by 24 pixels, and buttons at least 44 pixels tall on touch screens.
  • Dialogs: the screenshot viewer and these policies move focus in, keep it there, close on Escape and hand focus back to whatever opened them.
  • Without JavaScript: every part of the page, these policies included, can still be read and reached.

Known limitations

  • Long commands scroll sideways inside their own frame on narrow screens; the frame takes focus, so the arrow keys scroll it.
  • The screenshots are pictures of the app; their descriptions say what each shows, not every word in it. The app itself is the accessible version.
